# Nightpaw — Story Bible

> *Lose a penny, lose a key,*
> *lose a ribbon off a tree —*
> *all the things that slip from sight*
> *tumble Underneath at night.*
> *Hush now, hush, and hold on tight,*
> *or Underneath will keep you. Quite.*
>
> — a bedtime rhyme from Wickmoor

## Tone

Dark, creepy and cute at the same time. The world is full of lost things that have started to forget themselves, and that is sad and a bit frightening, but the characters are small, polite and funny. The creepiness comes from what is implied (empty slippers, a rhyme that knows too much, creatures that used to be someone's favorite toy), never from gore. Every scary thing has something tender underneath it.

**Rules of thumb:** no blood; enemies burst into dust, buttons and moth-light. Characters speak in short, odd, sincere sentences. Every area has one thing that is funny and one thing that is sad.

## The premise

**Nightpaw** is a small black cat with a red scarf. He belongs to **Mira Hale**, age seven, who lives in the rainy lamplit town of **Wickmoor**. Mira named him Nightpaw "because he walks around at night and his paws are the night."

Nightpaw is on his **ninth life**. He has lost the other eight over the years in small, sad, ordinary accidents. Mira doesn't know. Cats don't tell.

One stormy night Mira tells the old bedtime rhyme, falls asleep, and a pale moth taps on the window. In the morning the bed is empty, the window is open, and a trail of small muddy footprints leads across the garden to the **old well** that Mira was never, ever allowed near.

Nightpaw jumps in after her.

## The Underneath

At the bottom of the well is **the Underneath**: the place where lost things go. Lost buttons, lost socks, lost keys, lost pets, lost names. For centuries it was cozy down here. Everything was lit by the **Hearthlamp**, a great lantern at the center of the Underneath whose light kept lost things *remembering* what they were.

The Hearthlamp has gone out. Without its light, lost things slowly **go hollow**. They forget who they belonged to, then what they are, and they turn into restless, hungry shapes. That is where the enemies come from: every one of them was once someone's something.

The hollowing has a name down here: **the Hush**. It isn't a monster. It's what happens when everyone forgets.

## Why Mira was taken

The pale moth belongs to **Old Mothmother**, the last keeper of the Hearthlamp. Moths know about light. And Mothmother knows that the only light strong enough to relight the Hearthlamp is **the light of a child who still believes in things**. So she sent her moth to lead Mira down.

Mothmother isn't evil. She's desperate, and very old, and she has watched everything she loves go hollow. She means to put Mira inside the Hearthlamp. Mira would burn bright forever, and she would never go home.

## Nightpaw's eight lost lives

Every life a cat loses also ends up in the Underneath. Nightpaw's eight lost lives are scattered through the world as **Shades**: small, translucent ghost-kittens, each stuck in the memory of how that life ended.

Finding a Shade plays a short memory vignette and gives Nightpaw **one more paw of health**. In the game, **max health is the number of lives Nightpaw has remembered**.

## Endings

At the Hearthlamp, Nightpaw finds Mira asleep inside the great glass lantern, glowing softly. Mothmother is there, tiny and very tired. The Hush comes to put out the last light for good.

- **The Ninth Life ending** (fewer than 8 Shades): Nightpaw climbs into the Hearthlamp and trades places with Mira. A cat's eyes shine in the dark, after all. Mira wakes up in her own bed with a red scarf on her pillow. On the last screen the Hearthlamp glows, and in it are two yellow eyes that blink slowly. Bittersweet and a little creepy.
- **The Nine Lives ending** (all 8 Shades): Nightpaw gives the lamp one of his remembered lives instead of his last one. The Hearthlamp roars back to life, every Shade lights up like a candle, the hollow things remember themselves, and Nightpaw carries Mira home up the well at dawn. Last shot: Mira asleep, Nightpaw at the foot of the bed, and outside, a pale moth resting on the windowsill, doing no harm at all.

## Characters

**Nightpaw.** The player. In the storybook pages he's an ordinary black cat with a red scarf. Underneath, he walks upright, with his scarf pulled up over his muzzle and hanging down into a bell-shaped cloak that hides everything but his thin legs and his tail. Wide yellow eyes, whiskers poking out past the scarf. The scarf was Mira's; she wrapped it round him the first winter. Fights with his claws: a paw slips out from under the cloak and scratches. Never speaks; he reacts with ear twitches, tail flicks and slow blinks.

**Mira Hale.** Seven. Yellow boots, brave in the way small children are brave, which is to say by accident. Mostly seen in memories, and as echoes in the Underneath: her voice from far away, a slipper, drawings in chalk on the walls ("NIGHTPAW THIS WAY →").

**Tallow.** A moth. The Candlekeeper of the Hollows. She lights the candle shrines where Nightpaw rests (the save points). Nervous, formal and kind: "Good evening. I'm so sorry about everything." Was one of Mothmother's moths; left because she didn't agree with the plan, but is too polite to say so for a long time. After the Warden falls, she gives Nightpaw her late sister's wings (**Moth Wings**, the double jump).

**Nib.** A traveling mouse with a backpack full of buttons, which are money down here. A merchant and a cheerful liar. "Everything I sell is found, not lost. Totally different." Opens a shop in later areas; in the Hollows he's stuck behind a rockfall and just chats.

**The Hollow Warden.** Keeper of the Deep. Once the gentle gatekeeper of the well-mouth: a great antlered coat-stand in a moth-eaten coat, whose job was to catch falling lost things so they didn't break. Hollowed now, and attacks anything that falls. When defeated he remembers, briefly, and catches one last falling thing: Nightpaw.

**Old Mothmother.** Keeper of the Hearthlamp. Enormous wings, tiny voice. Heard long before she's seen.

**The Hush.** The final threat. Not a creature so much as a hole in the picture where things should be. Represented by silence in the music and a creeping black static at the edges of the screen.

## The world map (planned)

The Underneath is a vertical world: deeper means more forgotten.

1. **The Hollows** (Area 1, the demo). The bottom of the well: ash, roots, fallen stones, pale grass, candle shrines. Lost things that fell most recently. *Enemies:* Thimble Mites, Sock Wisps, Button Snails, Marble Toads. *Boss:* the Hollow Warden. *Ability:* Shadow Dash (found), Moth Wings (gift).
2. **The Drowned Nursery.** A flooded room of lost toys; music boxes that play by themselves. *Ability:* wall cling ("Velvet Claws").
3. **Mousewick Market.** A bustling town of mice who trade in lost buttons. Nib's shop. Funny area. *Ability:* the pounce.
4. **The Clockless Orchard.** Lost time: trees that grow clocks, stopped at the minute things were lost. *Ability:* the Thread (grapple).
5. **The Lampless Court.** The dark around the Hearthlamp. The Hush. Mothmother. The ending.
